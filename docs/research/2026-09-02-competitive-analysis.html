Competitive read · Remind Me Bills

The BNPL Gap

You are right that the bill-reminder market is crowded. You are wrong that it makes your app redundant — the crowd is standing somewhere else.

2 September 2026 Market: Philippines Focus: requirements & features

Saturated

Reminders, recurrence, calendars, mark-as-paid.

Solved by a dozen apps for a one-off $7.99 — and already inside GCash, GlobeOne and My Meralco, where Filipinos actually pay. Pay-period framing and no-bank-link privacy, which look like your differentiators, are competitors' existing marketing bullets.

Uncontested

One statement view across every BNPL provider a person uses.

SPayLater lives in Shopee, LazPayLater in Lazada, BillEase and Atome in their own apps — each on a different cycle convention. Nothing found consolidates them, and nothing found puts them beside an electricity bill.

01

The demand is a timing problem, not a credit problem

44% of Filipinos have used a BNPL service Fintech News PH
$8.90B PH BNPL volume in 2026, up 20.7% year on year → $18.81B by 2031
34–41% of BNPL users report at least one late payment Global
1.8–2% actually default (charge-offs) Global

Those last two figures are the whole argument for your app, and they only mean something side by side. Two in five BNPL users miss a payment. Fewer than one in fifty cannot pay. The gap between those numbers is people who had the money and lost track of the date.

That is not a lending problem or a budgeting problem. It is a reminder problem — which is the product you have already half-built.

It is also a fragmentation problem, and the PH market is unusually fragmented. A single shopper can plausibly hold SPayLater, LazPayLater, BillEase, Atome, GGives, GrabPayLater, Cashalo and TendoPay. Each tracks repayment only inside its own app, and the cycle rules do not agree with one another:

ProviderStatement cycleDue
SPayLater (Shopee)Cycle opens 5th or 25th, per user15th
LazPayLater (Lazada)1st → end of month16th

Two providers, two conventions, two apps, and a per-user variable in one of them. Add a third and a fourth and the mental arithmetic is where the 34–41% comes from.

03

Your app cannot currently remind anyone

Verified in the codebase

There is no notification delivery of any kind: no push, no email, no SMS, no cron or scheduled job, and no dependency capable of sending anything. vercel.json has no crons entry and src/app/api/ holds only auth/ and trpc/.

The app is a dashboard you must remember to open. For a product named Remind Me Bills, competing in a category whose entire value is arriving unprompted, this is the gap that matters more than every feature comparison above.

The competitive evidence makes this sharper. BillOut advertises 2M+ notifications sent as its headline proof of usefulness, with alerts up to 7 days ahead. GCash's bill reminder is a one-tap "Remind me Every" toggle. And the single most common complaint across reviews in this category is notifications that do not fire — which tells you notifications are not a feature of these products, they are the product, and users judge them on nothing else.

A tracker that requires the user to remember to check it is asking them to already have solved the problem it exists to solve.

04

Requirement gaps, in the order they cost you

  1. 01
    Reminder deliveryBlocking

    Push or email, scheduled, with a configurable lead time. Everything else in the product is downstream of this working. Table stakes for the category, and absent.

    Your app: none. Every competitor: yes.
  2. 02
    Semi-monthly pay periodsBlocking for PH

    Semi-monthly is the standard Philippine payroll schedule — the 15th and 30th, with 10th/25th and 5th/20th as common variants. Article 103 of the Labor Code requires intervals not exceeding 16 days, so it is close to universal.

    IncomeProfile.payFrequency offers weekly | fortnightly | monthly. Fortnightly is every 14 days — 26 pay dates a year, drifting through the calendar. Semi-monthly is 24 fixed dates. They are not interchangeable, and substituting one silently misplaces every bill in the pay-period view within two months.

    Your core differentiating view is calibrated to a pay cycle your likely market does not use.
  3. 03
    Amount due per period, totalledHigh demand

    A recurring review complaint is that apps show the bills but not the sum — users reach for a calculator to answer "what do I owe before my next payday". You already bucket bills by pay period and store amounts, so this is the cheapest high-value thing on the list.

    Structurally ready; not surfaced.
  4. 04
    Edit one occurrence, not the seriesHigh demand

    Reviewers ask for this explicitly: "do you want to change the payment just for this month or all of them?" Bills vary month to month — electricity especially — and changing one month currently means changing the rule.

    Your Payment model keys settled occurrences by occurrenceDate, but nothing stores a per-occurrence amount override.
  5. 05
    Mobile presenceStrategic

    BNPL purchases happen inside Shopee and Lazada, on a phone, impulsively. A web-only tracker sits the furthest possible distance from the moment a purchase needs recording — and manual entry is hardest to sustain exactly where your differentiator lives.

    Parked. Reasonable for now; not reasonable forever.
  6. 06
    Shared / household billsCompetitive parity

    BillOut leads with it and its reviews single it out ("share some of my bills with my partner"). Household cost-splitting is culturally routine in PH. Your Group model organizes bills but is single-user.

    Nice-to-have, but it is the feature competitors' users praise most.

That bottom row is the one to sit with. Reported medians below 0.2% in emerging markets mean a PH-targeted freemium finance app needs enormous reach before subscription revenue is real. It is not a coincidence that BillOut — the closest competitor by feature — chose a $7.99 one-off purchase rather than a subscription, and gives 60 days of premium before asking.

What Cushion's death does and does not tell you

Cushion was the one serious attempt at cross-provider BNPL aggregation — the self-described "Plaid for BNPL". It shut down in January 2025 after raising $21.6M, onboarding 1M consumers and reaching 200,000 paying customers, citing failure to reach sustaining scale.

Two opposite lessons sit in that sentence, and taking only one of them is the mistake. 200,000 people paid for this. The demand is not hypothetical. But what Cushion sold was automation — parsing 30 million emails and maintaining a live integration per provider — and that is what it could not afford. Your manual-entry design has none of that cost and none of that brittleness. It also has none of that convenience, which is the trade you are making whether or not you name it.

06

What I would do

  1. Stop competing as a bill reminder. That category is finished, cheap, and partly owned by the wallet app your users already have open. It is a feature you need, not a position you can hold.
  2. Ship notifications before anything else. Without them the differentiator cannot be demonstrated, because nobody opens a tracker unprompted.
  3. Add semi-monthly, and make it the default. A one-line union widening, and the difference between the pay-period view working and quietly lying for most Filipino users.
  4. Position on the BNPL statement, not on bills. "Every pay-later balance in one place, next to your actual bills, before payday" is a sentence no competitor found can say.
  5. Assume you cannot charge a subscription in this market. Design toward a one-off unlock, or accept it as a portfolio piece and optimize for what you learn instead.

Your instinct that the market is crowded is correct and worth trusting. It is crowded in front of the door you were not planning to walk through.
